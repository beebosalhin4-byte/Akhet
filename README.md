# Two-Eyes-of-Hourus
In myth, the Sun and Moon were the two eyes of Horus. Two Eyes of Horus is a browser-based tool for rapid evaluation of lunar landing sites. Each site is judged by two “eyes”: the Sun eye for power and the Moon eye for the direct line to Earth. Users choose from 16 curated sites, including Apollo, CLPS and south pole targets, or enter any custom lunar coordinates.
Sun and Earth positions come from the astronomy-engine ephemeris (about 1 arcminute accuracy), rotated into the Moon’s frame using the IAU 2009 pole and liberation. For each of 360 azimuths, the tool computes a terrain skyline from LOLA topography out to 200 km, including the Moon’s curvature, with no atmospheric refraction.
It reports solar elevation, relative power, sunny share, Earth-in-view share, longest night, longest no-signal gap, and countdowns to sunset and Earthset over a 30-day window. A first-person surface view, a 3D space map, a horizon panorama and a clickable timeline make these results easy to read. Side-by-side comparison lets planners test alternative sites or dates in seconds. Rigorous enough for engineers and clear enough for the public, it shows at a glance where both eyes stay open.
