Two Eyes of Horus

In ancient Egypt, the sky god Horus had two eyes.

The right eye was the Sun: blazing light, the giver of life.

The left eye was the Moon: wounded, healed, and always watching over the night.

Where earth meets sky they placed Akhet, the horizon, where light is born and where it dies.

At the Moon's south pole the Sun creeps along Akhet and never climbs. Your Sun Eye is power, your Moon Eye is the line home to Earth. Find the landing site where both stay open.

What do these words mean?

Moon day vs Earth day

All timers here use Earth days (24 hours each). One Moon day, from one sunrise to the next, lasts about 29.5 Earth days: roughly two weeks of daylight, then two weeks of night.

Sun elevation

How high the Sun is above the horizon. 90° is straight up. At the Moon's south pole it never goes above about 1.5°, so sunlight arrives almost sideways and tall hills block it.

Horizon map

The top picture shows what you would see standing there: the ground's skyline (the dark shape) all the way around, with the Sun and Earth. The Moon has no air, so there is no glow or bending of light: when something drops behind the skyline, it is simply gone.

Sunset and Earthset timers

Hours or days until the Sun or Earth slips behind the skyline (or comes back). One Moon day lasts about 29.5 Earth days, so a sunset can mean a two-week night.

Power and communication

Solar panels make power only while the Sun is up, and more when it is higher. A lander can talk straight to Earth only while Earth is above the skyline. Move the date by one day and watch how much changes.

Why does the Earth wobble?

The Moon nods and swings a little as it orbits (called libration), so Earth moves slightly in the sky. The app includes this.
